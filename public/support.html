Deposit Kit

Help

Deposit Kit helps you record what a rental looked like: a home when you move in, or a rental car when you pick it up and return it. Then you can show what was already there if anyone charges you for it. It’s free, and your photos stay on your device.

How do I use it?

  1. Add your home’s address, move-in date and state.
  2. Go room by room. Photograph walls, floors, windows, doors and appliances, and get close to anything already damaged. Add a short note like “scratch on floor by window”.
  3. Tap Save my record. Save both the PDF report and the zip file somewhere safe, such as your email or cloud storage.

How do I use it for a rental car?

  1. Before you drive off, tap Rental car and enter the car or its plate. The rest can wait.
  2. The app asks for one photo at a time: front, both sides, back, glass, wheels, seats, trunk, and the dashboard showing mileage and fuel. Photograph any damage up close with a note.
  3. When you return the car, open the same record and tap Start return photos. Take the same photos at the drop-off spot before you leave. The app shows your pickup photo of each spot so you can match the angle.
  4. Save the PDF and zip. Keep them for a few months, because damage charges can arrive weeks after a rental.

Why do I need to save my record?

The app keeps your photos only on this device. Phones can clear app data, and iPhones may delete a website’s data if you don’t open it for 7 days. The saved files are your lasting copy.

I got a new phone, or my photos disappeared

Open Deposit Kit and tap I have a saved record (or Restore from a saved file at the bottom of the list of your records), then choose the zip file you saved. Your rooms or car photos, notes and timestamps come back, next to any records already on the device.

What does “timestamped” mean?

A public timestamp service signed a fingerprint of the photo with the time. If anyone later changes the photo, even slightly, the check fails. The photo itself is never uploaded; only its fingerprint is sent.

A photo says it’s waiting for a timestamp

The photo is saved. The timestamp needs an internet connection, and the app tries again automatically when you’re back online. You can also tap Try again.

How can someone check my photos?

The zip file includes the original photos, a timestamp file for each one, and instructions. Anyone with OpenSSL (built into Mac and Linux) can check a photo against its timestamp. The PDF report explains how.

Can Deposit Kit tell me what my rights are?

No. Deposit Kit gives you templates and deadlines, not legal advice. Check your state’s law or a tenant rights group if you’re unsure.
